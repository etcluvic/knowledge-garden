# Artificial intelligence-assisted tools for redefining the communication landscape of the scholarly world

**Razack, H. I., Mathew, Sam T., Saad, F. F. A; Alqahtani, S** (2021)

*Science Editing*

🔗 [https://doi.org/10.6087/kcse.244](https://doi.org/10.6087/kcse.244)

---

## Annotation

Razack et al. argue AI will transform publishing into a technology-driven industry, streamlining dissemination of scholarship “for the betterment of humankind.” Exploring current trends via case studies, they emphasize AI’s impact upon human performance over its independent “creative” role. They argue AI could benefit the “prospective” researcher by predicting an article’s “citation impact”, identifying evolving research trends, and locating relevant research journals to publish in, cutting past low-charging and “predatory OA journals” which “malign integrity” by publishing “compromised content.” Meanwhile, “retrospective” editors could employ AI to detect plagiarism, streamline peer review, and format publications. For Razack et al., the rise of AI can therefore be compared to the rise of the typewriter, swift adoption likely promoting ‘human-machine ation’ over replacement.
